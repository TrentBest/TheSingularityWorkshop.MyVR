01 · The transformation

From another game of laser tag
to a place for new adventures.

Imagine a familiar arena. The walls, doors, routes, cover, and safe movement areas stay real. The virtual layer gives that same space a new identity: a reactor breach, an alien outpost, a dungeon, or a team mission. This is the product direction we want to prove—not a claim that every capability is shipping today.

START WITH WHAT EXISTS

Your arena is the foundation

Physical topology, routes, obstacles, staff procedures, and safety constraints remain the source of truth.

ADD A SHARED WORLD

Give the group a reason to play

Team identity, objectives, mission state, virtual effects, and coordinated encounters can build on the same space.

REUSE THE FOUNDATION

Change the story, not the building

New authored Experiences can reinterpret a venue without requiring a different physical arena for every theme.

02 · Business value

More ways to fill the calendar.
More reasons to return.

A venue does not need to become a game studio to explore new group experiences. The commercial opportunity is to make existing space more versatile and create new bookable formats. These are operator revenue opportunities to validate with pilot venues—not guaranteed outcomes.

EVERYDAY PLAY

Core group sessions

Offer themed rounds and rotating missions within the venue's existing booking rhythm.

PREMIUM BOOKINGS

Private events

Build packages around birthdays, corporate groups, team competitions, and special occasions.

REPEAT VISITS

Leagues and campaigns

Explore scheduled tournaments, recurring story arcs, LARP sessions, and persistent character progression.

What the venue could earn from

  • Admissions and timed sessions
  • Premium missions or themed event packages
  • Private group and corporate bookings
  • Leagues, tournaments, and recurring campaigns
  • Food, merchandise, and other existing venue add-ons

What the platform might charge for

  • Venue or platform subscription
  • Per-device, per-seat, or per-session usage
  • Experience licensing or creator revenue share
  • Optional hosted persistence for ongoing campaigns
  • Paid founding-venue pilots or co-development

These are alternative commercial hypotheses. Pricing, billing mechanics, creator economics, and hosted persistence are not yet announced products.

03 · One venue, many experiences

Start with laser tag.
Then open up the world.

The first proof should be small and familiar. Once a venue's physical topology and group session are represented, new authored content can explore other ways to play.

FIRST STEP

Laser-tag extension

Virtual beams, impacts, shields, team identity, and objectives layered onto a familiar arena.

NEXT THEMES

Sci-fi insertion

Boarding actions, alien facilities, reactor missions, and squad-based objectives.

NEW FORMATS

Dungeons and escape rooms

Transform routes and rooms into shared puzzles, encounters, and tactical adventures.

RECURRING PLAY

LARP and campaigns

Explore weekly sessions with persistent characters, quests, and progression—if the hosting model is validated.

EVENT CALENDAR

Seasonal worlds

Limited-time themes and group events that refresh the offer without rebuilding the venue.

CREATOR ECOSYSTEM

More than one studio

A future creator/venue model could let multiple authors contribute reusable experiences, subject to publishing and licensing design.

04 · How it works

A platform with clear responsibilities.

The venue, the shared Experience, and the headset each have different jobs. Keeping those boundaries clear is how one experience can travel across devices without every client becoming its own game engine or content platform.

The venue

Physical space, layout, routes, equipment, operations, and safety constraints.

The Experience

Authored world meaning, mission rules, shared entities, and canonical identity.

MyVR

Headset/device capabilities, immersive interaction, and each participant's local view.

Visualize

Presentation execution and visual backend/resource/frame work.

05 · For venue operators

Start with your venue,
not a technology checklist.

A useful pilot begins by understanding the business and the physical space. We want to learn how your groups play today, how your venue is operated, and what would make a new experience worth offering.

What we would map together

  • Venue size, layout, routes, and capacity
  • Current game systems, props, and player equipment
  • Group size, session length, and staff workflow
  • Safety procedures and physical constraints
  • Network, device, and operational requirements
  • What would make customers book again

What a first proof should answer

  • Can the existing arena support a compelling virtual layer?
  • Can a group share one coherent mission?
  • Can the same physical space support more than one mode?
  • Can staff operate the session reliably?
  • Does the experience create measurable customer value?

This page is an early business-facing draft. A public pilot/contact intake channel and commercial offer are not yet configured.

06 · Understand today's options

How do venues get new content today?

We are investigating the current content-supply landscape rather than assuming every operator buys the same way. A venue may build internally, commission a studio, license a catalog, rely on a system vendor, or run a traditional laser-tag stack. The right comparison starts with operator interviews and named, sourced examples.

In-house creation

What staff, skills, tools, and maintenance burden does a venue need to own?

Commissioned work

Who creates bespoke experiences, who owns updates, and how are changes priced?

Licensed catalogs

What distributors or platform vendors exist, what do they provide, and what flexibility does the venue retain?

No market leader, competitor, typical price, or dominant operating model is claimed on this draft page. Those claims require current evidence.
