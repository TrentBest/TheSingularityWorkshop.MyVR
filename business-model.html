MyVR · Business model working paper

Build the economics
from the ground up.

The goal is not to force the business to support a predetermined salary or fee. It is to discover what the ecosystem actually needs: operating costs, partner economics, reinvestment, a cash buffer, optional owner compensation, and sustainable profit.

Model status: all adoption, revenue, and cost values in the calculator are editable illustrative inputs—not market facts, quotes, commitments, or a forecast. The $165,000 compensation figure discussed in research is context only, not a required salary or business expense. Owner compensation starts at $0 in this model and is entirely optional.

01 · Four separate economic layers

Follow value before assigning a fee.

Venue revenue

Customer-facing business

Admissions, group sessions, parties, corporate events, leagues, food, merchandise, and other venue sales. This is the venue's business revenue—not automatically Workshop revenue.

Distributor revenue

Local customer relationship

Installation, equipment, onboarding, training, deployment, local support, and account management. The distributor should earn the larger share for doing this work.

Creator revenue

Experience supply

Creators may earn from licensed, commissioned, or premium experiences under explicit rights, maintenance, and revenue-share terms.

Workshop receipts

Platform, distribution, and optional services

Potential receipts include a venue subscription, attributable usage fee, deployment/service fee, hosting or persistence, analytics, premium support, and a creator-platform fee. The fee base must match the value delivered and be auditable.

Do not apply a platform percentage to all gross ticket revenue by default. Ticket sales include venue labor, rent, equipment, marketing, taxes, and other value created outside the Workshop platform. Start with attributable platform/content revenue or a clear service fee; test a broader base only if the value and attribution justify it.

03 · What the calculator is—and isn't—saying

Separate the milestones.

Milestone A

Operating break-even

Recurring Workshop receipts cover variable and fixed operating costs. This is survival of the service, not proof of a healthy ecosystem.

Milestone B

Reinvestment and resilience

Receipts also fund a reserve, maintenance, security, product development, partner enablement, and measured growth.

Milestone C

Optional owner compensation

Compensation becomes a variable the business can support after costs and reserves. It need not match any external salary benchmark.

Milestone D

Meaningful profit

Profit is what remains after real costs, taxes and obligations are accounted for—not the same thing as gross platform receipts or a nominal percentage allocation.

A proposed 30% total charge should be evaluated only after defining the base. A 30% fee on a narrowly attributable software/content amount is economically different from 30% of venue ticket sales. Compare the partner's total take, the creator's share, payment processing, Workshop costs, and venue value in every case.

05 · Partner architecture and governance

Scale distribution without surrendering control.

Anchor distributor responsibilities

  • Own local prospecting and customer relationships.
  • Coordinate sales, installation, onboarding and training.
  • Provide first-line support and venue feedback.
  • Operate a partner-branded domain and scoped workspace.
  • Manage only deployments and tenants it is authorized to manage.

Workshop responsibilities

  • Steward core technology and platform evolution.
  • Define technical standards, release controls and central escalation.
  • Provide deployment authorization, audit and observability.
  • Define licensing, creator rights and billing attribution.
  • Support export, transfer and partner offboarding.

Minimum controls before a multi-venue commercial launch

Tenant isolation

Partner and venue scopes cannot expose unrelated customer data or deployments.

Deployment lifecycle

Authorized rollout, version visibility, rollback, health monitoring and audit records.

Commercial attribution

Explain who earned what, from which service or event, with a reconciliation path.

Branded domains, distributor workspaces, multi-venue controls, billing, and hosted persistence are proposed architecture—not capabilities this page claims are already implemented. Actual hardware and Quest support remain unproven until tested on target devices.

06 · Adoption strategy and validation

CiAi can accelerate the funnel. It cannot guarantee demand.

The accelerated scenario represents a deliberate target: use CiAi and coordinated development to reduce the time required to onboard venues and partners. It is a hypothesis to stress-test alongside slower adoption. The model should be updated with actual conversion, onboarding time, churn, support load, and collected revenue as evidence arrives.

Prove one anchor venue

Map one real arena, run staff-supervised sessions, validate safety and throughput, and measure customer response.

Repeat the deployment

Show that a distributor can deploy a second and third venue with lower effort and without cross-tenant access.

Measure unit economics

Track actual onboarding labor, support tickets, hosting, payment fees, partner margin, repeat bookings, and retained monthly revenue.

Evidence to collect before setting public pricing

  • At least one real venue's attributable platform/content revenue and operating costs.
  • Time and cost to onboard, train, deploy, update, and support each venue.
  • Distributor's actual sales cycle, acquisition cost, installation cost, and margin.
  • Usage distribution across venues, including quiet months and seasonal demand.
  • Retention, renewal, repeat bookings, refunds, and service-level expectations.
  • Creator economics and the cost of keeping experiences maintained.

The first goal is not to maximize the fee. It is to find a model where the venue earns incremental value, the distributor is rewarded for local work, creators can participate fairly, and the Workshop can operate and improve the platform sustainably.
